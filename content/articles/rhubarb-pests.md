One way to deter them from nibbling in your flower beds is to overpower their sense of smell. Take a bar of strong smelling soap (Irish Spring, for example), cut it up into small pieces (or shred it in a food processor), and sprinkle the soap around your flower beds. The soap won't harm your plants, but it will help to keep the deer at bay. Apply in the early spring, just when the tulip leaves begin to pop up, and again in the fall just before the first snow. <http://lymanorchards.com/critters.htm> Squirrels   |  | | --- | | [rwd.book.gif (11790 bytes)](http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/ASIN/1556523025/therhubarbcompen)Outwitting Squirrels:   101 Cunning Stratagems to Reduce Dramatically the  Egregious Misappropriation of Seed from Your   Birdfeeder by Squirrels |   Plant interactionsBlack Walnut Several people have written me and mentioned that their rhubarb does not grow near walnut trees. It seems that the roots of Black Walnut (*Juglans nigra* L.) and Butternut (*Juglans cinerea* L.) produce a  juglone (5-hydroxy-alphanapthaquinone).  Many plants such as tomato, potato, blackberry, blueberry, azalea, mountain laurel, rhododendron, **rhubarb**, red pine and apple may be injured or killed within one to two months of growth within the root zone of these trees. Other plants remain unaffected (like Clematis, Hybiscus,  Viburnum,   Squashes, Melons, Beans, Carrots, Corn and marigold to name a few). The toxic zone from a mature tree occurs on average in a 50 to 60 foot radius from the trunk, but can be up to 80 feet.
